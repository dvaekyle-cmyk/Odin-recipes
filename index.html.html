Chicken Adobo

Description

Adobo is often called the unofficial national dish of the Philippines. Chicken is simmered in soy sauce, vinegar, garlic, bay leaves and peppercorns until tender, giving a salty, tangy and savory sauce that goes perfectly with steamed rice.

Recipe Information

Quick facts for Chicken Adobo
Preparation TimeCooking TimeServingsDifficulty
15 minutes (plus 30 minutes marinating)40 minutes4 to 6Easy

Ingredients

  • 1 kg chicken thighs or drumsticks
  • 1/2 cup soy sauce
  • 1/2 cup white or cane vinegar
  • 1 head garlic, peeled and crushed
  • 3 dried bay leaves
  • 1 teaspoon whole black peppercorns
  • 1 cup water
  • 2 tablespoons cooking oil
  • 1 tablespoon brown sugar (optional)

Instructions

  1. Marinate the chicken in the soy sauce and half of the garlic for 30 minutes.
  2. Heat the oil and sauté the remaining garlic until lightly golden.
  3. Add the chicken and brown each side for about 3 minutes.
  4. Pour in the marinade, vinegar and water. Add the bay leaves and peppercorns.
  5. Bring to a boil without stirring for 2 minutes, then simmer covered for 30 minutes.
  6. Simmer uncovered for 5 to 10 minutes until the sauce thickens. Serve with steamed rice.

Sinigang na Baboy

Description

Sinigang is a comforting Filipino soup known for its sour broth, traditionally made with tamarind. Tender pork and fresh vegetables are cooked together, making it a favorite on rainy days.

Recipe Information

Quick facts for Sinigang na Baboy
Preparation TimeCooking TimeServingsDifficulty
15 minutes1 hour 15 minutes4 to 6Medium

Ingredients

  • 1 kg pork belly or ribs, cut into pieces
  • 8 cups water
  • 1 large tomato, quartered
  • 1 medium onion, quartered
  • 1 pack (40 g) sinigang mix, or about 10 fresh tamarind fruits
  • 2 long green chili peppers
  • 1 small white radish, sliced
  • 1 eggplant, sliced
  • 8 string beans, cut into 2-inch pieces
  • 1 bunch water spinach (kangkong)
  • Fish sauce and salt to taste

Instructions

  1. Boil the water and add the pork, onion and tomato.
  2. Simmer covered for 45 to 60 minutes until the pork is tender. Skim off any scum.
  3. Stir in the sinigang mix (or the boiled, mashed tamarind).
  4. Add the radish and cook 5 minutes, then add the eggplant, string beans and chili for 5 more minutes.
  5. Season with fish sauce and salt, add the kangkong and turn off the heat. Serve hot with rice.

Leche Flan

Description

Leche flan is a rich and creamy Filipino custard made with egg yolks and milk, topped with sweet caramel. It is a classic dessert at Christmas, birthdays and fiestas.

Recipe Information

Quick facts for Leche Flan
Preparation TimeCooking TimeServingsDifficulty
20 minutes (plus 4 hours chilling)50 minutes6 to 8Medium

Ingredients

  • 10 egg yolks
  • 1 can (14 oz) condensed milk
  • 1 can (12 oz) evaporated milk
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 3/4 cup white sugar (for the caramel)
  • Aluminum foil
  • 1 llanera (oval mold) or round baking dish

Instructions

  1. Melt the sugar in the mold over low heat until golden brown. Tilt to coat the bottom and let it harden.
  2. Gently mix the egg yolks, condensed milk, evaporated milk and vanilla without making bubbles.
  3. Strain the mixture twice, then pour into the caramel-coated mold and cover tightly with foil.
  4. Steam on low heat for 40 to 50 minutes until set.
  5. Cool, then chill at least 4 hours. Run a knife around the edge, flip onto a plate and serve.
